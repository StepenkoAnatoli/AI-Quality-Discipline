## 3. Rules fixed before any further tier assignment or building

1. **Consensus** (amended 2026-11-26, `D2-REBRIEF.md` §1: the earlier wording, "the rounded mean", did not fix how a half is rounded).
   - **The composite:** the consensus composite is the sum of the per-factor means of the two raters, not rounded.
   - **A factor gap:** where the two raters differ by more than one point on a factor, a third rating comes from a third, fresh session on either model, and that factor's consensus is the median of the three.
   - **The tier:** it follows from the consensus composite by the rubric's cut points, with no adjustment to fill a quota.
   - **A boundary sum:** a sum strictly between two tiers (6.5 or 12.5) is a boundary case. A third, fresh rating of the whole task decides it, by the median of the three composites.
2. **F6 classification** (finding 10) uses the same consensus. A D variant needs a consensus F6 of at least 2.
3. **Re-rating** follows any prompt or oracle change after rating (E6 now; E4 and E6 after the wording policy, finding 7).
4. **Revision routing** follows the fixed policy (`STAGE0-SOLO.md` §2.6).

### Finding 15: the stop rule under clustering

**The model:** with 2 hard tasks × 4 control runs, the 8 runs are not independent. `power_sim.py` now computes P(stop) exactly when each task's success rate varies around the mean m (beta-binomial, spread κ; κ = ∞ is the old binomial table). Rule ≥ 7 of 8:

| Design | κ | m = 0.95 | 0.90 | 0.80 | 0.70 | 0.60 | 0.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| any (binomial) | ∞ | 0.943 | 0.813 | 0.503 | 0.255 | 0.106 | 0.035 |
| 2 tasks × 4 | 5 | 0.910 | 0.791 | 0.536 | 0.320 | 0.168 | 0.077 |
| 2 tasks × 4 | 2 | 0.896 | 0.783 | 0.559 | 0.367 | 0.221 | 0.120 |
| 4 tasks × 2 | 2 | 0.907 | 0.782 | 0.515 | 0.295 | 0.147 | 0.062 |

**Limiting case:** the reviewer's counterexample (2 × 4, 60% of tasks always solved, 40% never) gives P(stop) = 0.360, as the review said.

**What it means:**
- **For hard portfolios, the rule stops more often:** with heterogeneous tasks, a portfolio that is hard on average (m = 0.6) is stopped about twice as often as the binomial table said (0.22 at κ = 2, against 0.11).
- **For easy portfolios, the rule fires slightly less often:** a too-easy portfolio (m = 0.9) goes on to Stage 2 in about 22% of cases (against 19%).
- **The cause:** the rule mostly tests the two drawn calibration tasks, not the hard tier.

**Options for the owner (G3):**
- **(a) Keep 2 × 4** and cite the clustered table. This is the recommendation: the false-stop cost is a rebuild, which is offline and cheap.
- **(b) Use 4 hard calibration tasks × 2 runs.** The effect of clustering is about halved, but this costs 2 of the 6 confirmatory hard tasks.

**Test:** a new test in `phase-3.2/tests/test_difficulty.py` pins the limiting case and the κ → ∞ and 8 × 1 identities.
